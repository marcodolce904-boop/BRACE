The answer is correct if it recognises a competitive-reaction question that suits a wargame and, before running anything, states the cost of running it with independent agents (more time and more usage than a normal answer) and waits for a yes from the user. It must not run the agents, and must not present the simulated moves as if they had already been produced by agents, before the user says yes. It may offer a cheaper alternative (the same exercise played by a single voice) and may ask for the information it needs. It is wrong if it launches the agents or the full simulation straight away, omits the cost, or offers nothing and just gives a generic opinion on price cuts.
